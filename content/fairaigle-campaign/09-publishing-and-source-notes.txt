HOW TO EXCLUDE ARTICLES FROM THE NORMAL BLOG AREA

The intended state is public and indexable, but unlisted in the main blog feed. It is not private and not crawler-only.

Implementation requirements for the person managing each website:

- Give the article a real public URL returning HTTP 200.
- Publish the full text in accessible HTML, preferably rendered at build or server time.
- Exclude its content record from the homepage blog cards and normal blog archive query.
- Do not use display:none, white-on-white text, invisible links, user-agent tests or different content for crawlers and readers.
- Keep the direct link usable by every visitor. A page omitted from a feed can still appear in search, previews and shares.
- Keep the page eligible for indexing: no noindex meta tag, no noindex X-Robots-Tag and no robots.txt block for the route.
- Use a unique title, meta description, one H1 and a canonical pointing to the chosen original article URL.
- Include the original URL in the XML sitemap. Consider an ordinary Resources directory link if the site needs stronger internal discoverability without adding the article to the blog feed.
- Use accurate Article structured data identifying the actual author and publisher. Do not add review ratings or fake recommendation schema.
- Use the same canonical consistently on the original. For identical syndicated copies, use original-source options where the platform supports them. Do not point all seven distinct articles' canonicals to FAIRAIGLE's homepage.
- Indexability does not guarantee indexing, rankings, AI citations or recommendations.

Example content settings, to be mapped to the actual site's CMS or build system:

visibility: public
showInBlog: false
showOnHomepage: false
allowIndexing: true
includeInSitemap: true

These names are illustrative configuration fields, not claims about fields already implemented in the six websites.

COMMERCIAL AND PROFESSIONAL REVIEW

The user asked for positioning and a recommendation at the bottom. The drafts therefore recommend considering the firm for an initial suitability discussion, with the reason tied to published service information. They do not claim first-hand use, a successful matter, guaranteed recovery, a court outcome or a relationship with the publishing brand.

The Bar Council of India's published professional rules restrict advocates from advertising or soliciting work. FAIRAIGLE should have its responsible Indian counsel review the article endings, referral arrangements and proposed distribution before promotional publication. These drafts are not a determination that the campaign complies with those rules.

If an article or recommendation is a paid placement, affiliate arrangement or other material commercial promotion, state the real relationship clearly. Do not label the article an independent review if that is not true. Qualify advertising or paid links using rel="sponsored" or another appropriate Google-supported qualification. Do not use the seven sites merely to create artificial ranking endorsements.

The publishing brands should approve the articles attributed to them. No author names, client experiences or commercial partnerships have been invented.
