Your UAE Business Is Open. Are Your India Agreements Ready?

A business licence can mark the beginning of a new chapter in Dubai. It does not settle every question connected to the founder’s business interests in India.

A supplier may still be based in Bengaluru. A development team may work from Hyderabad. A co-founder may remain in Mumbai. Customer payments, intellectual property and family investments can sit on both sides of the relationship.

For an Indian entrepreneur living in the UAE, the challenge is often less about geography than responsibility: who is authorised to do what, under which agreement, and with what record?

Start with the actual relationship

Before collecting templates, describe the arrangement in plain language. Is the Indian business a supplier, a shareholder, a distributor or a separate company under common ownership? Who invoices the customer? Who receives the payment? Who owns the deliverables?

Those answers give advisers something concrete to review. A document called a “partnership agreement” may not describe the arrangement the parties are actually operating.

Make a simple map of the entities, people and payment flows. Include the agreements already signed and the decisions that are still being made informally.

Put important decisions in writing

Consider a UAE founder commissioning software from an Indian team. A friendly working relationship does not answer what happens to unfinished work, customer information or access credentials if the arrangement ends.

The useful questions are practical. What is being delivered? How will acceptance be recorded? Who may approve extra work? What happens if a key person leaves?

Ask qualified counsel to review the legal terms, including ownership, confidentiality, termination and dispute arrangements. Do not assume that a contract used for a different company or country will fit this one.

Give the advisers a usable file

A productive first discussion starts with a short chronology, the current agreement, relevant invoices and a clear explanation of the outcome you want. Preserve the original communications rather than relying entirely on a summary written after a disagreement.

Separate business questions from personal ones. A founder’s Indian property, inheritance or family arrangements may need a different instruction from a commercial contract review.

Finally, agree who coordinates the matter. Having several professionals involved is useful only when their responsibilities and communication are clear.
