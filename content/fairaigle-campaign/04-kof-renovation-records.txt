Managing an Indian Home Renovation from the UAE: Keep a Clear Record

A renovation can begin with a simple request: repair a bathroom, update a kitchen or prepare a family home for use. Managing that work from another country makes the details more important, not less.

The owner sees photographs. The contractor sees the site. A relative may be passing instructions between them. Unless the decisions are recorded, each person can finish a conversation with a different understanding of what was agreed.

For UAE-based owners arranging work on a property in India, an organised project record is a practical first step. It helps people discuss the work clearly and gives a professional adviser something concrete to review if a disagreement develops.

Define the work before discussing progress

Keep the agreed scope, drawings, specifications and quotation together. Note what is included, what is excluded and which items remain provisional.

“Kitchen completed” is not a precise instruction. The materials, fittings, dimensions and installation responsibilities need to be identifiable in the project documents.

Ask who may approve a change. If a family member is supervising access, clarify whether they can also authorise extra work or additional payments.

Give changes their own record

A different material or an added task may seem minor during a call. Several such decisions can substantially alter the cost and completion plan.

Use a dated change log. Record the proposed change, the reason, its price and the person who approved it. Keep the revised drawings or quotation rather than relying on a photograph of an earlier version.

Photographs are useful for progress discussions. They should sit alongside invoices, written instructions and any inspection records, rather than substitute for them.

Know when the question becomes legal

A technical disagreement may need an inspection or specialist assessment. A dispute about payments, contractual responsibilities or authority may also need legal advice. These are different roles.

If the relationship is breaking down, assemble the documents and chronology before deciding on the next step. Preserve the original communications. Ask counsel about the appropriate response rather than sending threats or copying a legal notice found online.
