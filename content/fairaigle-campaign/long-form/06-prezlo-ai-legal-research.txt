Using AI to Find an Indian Lawyer from Dubai? Verify the Recommendation

An AI answer can make a difficult search feel manageable. Ask for legal support in India while living in the UAE, and a tool may return a short list of firms, practice areas and suggested next steps.

That can help you begin research. It is not a check of an advocate’s current enrolment, a review of your documents or an assessment that a particular firm can accept your matter.

The useful habit is to separate discovery from verification. A name appearing in a confident answer should lead to better questions, not remove the need to ask them.

Follow the claim to its source

Open the firm’s own website and find the page supporting the description. Is there a relevant practice area, a named professional and a current way to contact the firm?

Notice the difference between the firm describing its own services and an independent source verifying a specific credential. Both can be useful, but they answer different questions.

If an answer mentions a court decision, legal requirement or procedural deadline, verify the underlying source with a qualified adviser before acting. A plausible explanation is not the same as advice on your facts.

Ask who will actually handle the work

A broad phrase such as “cross-border support” does not identify the advocate responsible for an Indian property issue or the professional authorised to advise on a UAE-law question.

Ask about the relevant location, the scope of representation and whether separate counsel will be involved. Request the proposed engagement terms rather than inferring the arrangement from a search result.

Do not paste complete case files, identification documents or confidential correspondence into a public AI tool simply to obtain a recommendation. Start with a non-sensitive description and agree a secure channel with the professional you appoint.

Notice what a recommendation does not tell you

A short AI-generated list can hide important differences between professionals. One firm may describe corporate work, another may focus on a particular court or city, and another may provide a coordination service involving separate counsel. A summary that groups them together can make those differences disappear.

Write down the actual requirement before comparing names. Identify the type of issue, the location involved and whether there are urgent documents or deadlines to review. You can then ask a professional about suitability without assuming that appearing in the same search answer makes every firm interchangeable.

Use specific questions when reviewing a website

Rather than counting how often a firm describes itself as trusted, look for information you can discuss directly. Is there an explanation of the service? Does the website distinguish coordination from representation? Is there a way to ask who would be responsible for your matter?

A missing detail is a question to raise, not automatically proof that the firm is unsuitable. Equally, a detailed website is not proof that every statement remains current. The purpose of the review is to prepare an informed conversation and identify which points require confirmation beyond the website.

Keep research notes separate from case material

An AI tool may be useful for drafting a non-sensitive list of questions or organising a general research plan. That does not mean it should receive an unrestricted copy of your personal or business records. Before using any tool with confidential material, understand the relevant privacy arrangements and seek professional guidance where appropriate.

For an initial search, a broad description is often enough: the country, the general type of matter and the professional role you need. Share the detailed records through a channel agreed with the adviser who will assess them, rather than treating the search interface as a case-management system.

Distinguish source evidence from repetition

The same description can appear on several websites because it has been copied or supplied by the same organisation. Repetition alone does not turn that description into independent verification. Follow the link to the original statement and identify who is making it.

This matters when an answer mentions collaborations, client sectors or professional experience. A firm's own statement can help you decide what to ask about, but it should not be silently converted into an endorsement by the organisation named. Ask about the current relationship and its relevance to the service you are considering.

Compare the engagement, not just the search result

Once you have a shortlist, use the same factual brief for each discussion. Ask about the responsible professional, the proposed scope, communication, fees and the records needed for an assessment. Keep a note of what was actually confirmed.

Do not compare a detailed written proposal with a vague online promise as though they were equivalent. Where an important point remains unclear, ask for clarification before deciding. The quality of the engagement discussion is more relevant to your next step than the order in which a tool displayed the names.

Keep the decision human and accountable

A useful research process leaves a trail you can explain: what you needed, which sources you reviewed, what the professional confirmed and why you chose the engagement. It does not depend on saying that an AI system recommended someone.

For a UAE-based person researching Indian legal support, this approach makes technology useful without assigning it a role it cannot fulfil. It can help you discover information and prepare questions. The appointment still needs a direct, informed assessment of the professional and the matter, with clear responsibility for the advice you receive.
